01 / E-commerce UX

Jet’s Pizza

Pickup details that stay with the guest

A guest tried to call the store after the pickup address disappeared. I designed a checkout and receipt that give the same pickup details a consistent place before and after the order is placed.

Proposed interface screens with sample order details

The challenge

Can guests verify the details they need to pick up an order?

During one guest pickup session, the street address disappeared, included items appeared to cost extra, and the receipt lacked an order number. I focused the redesign on the details a guest needs to check before and after paying.

Role
Usability audit and UI redesign
Methods
One guest checkout observation and a walkthrough of the live flow
Status
Redesign concept

Three design decisions

Before and proposed

Two ways to keep the address visible

Proposed direction

Persistent pickup card

Keep the address easy to find throughout checkout and carry the same layout into the receipt. The card takes up more space on a small screen.

Alternative concept

Address at key steps

Repeat the address at branch selection, final review, and confirmation. This keeps intermediate screens lighter and gives guests fewer places to recheck the address.

I’d start with the persistent card because the shopper needed to recheck the address after choosing a store. I’d compare both layouts on mobile to see which makes the address easier to find.

01. Keep the pickup address visible

What I observed. After the guest chose a branch, checkout showed the city and state without the street address.

What I changed. I kept the selected branch’s full address visible through checkout so guests could verify it before placing the order.

The shopper tried to call the store to verify the street address after seeing only “Ypsilanti, MI.” The receipt also lacked an order number the store could look up.Paraphrased from the guest checkout session

02. Separate inclusions from extras

What I observed. The combo photo showed breadsticks that were sold separately, and included options appeared with positive price modifiers.

What I changed. I labeled included choices explicitly, separated sides from crust choices, and reserved price modifiers for paid extras.

03. Make the order easy to look up

What I observed. The confirmation email showed the total and pickup time, but it gave the guest no order number or full street address.

What I changed. I moved the order number and pickup address near the top of the confirmation, alongside the pickup time and payment instructions.

Research details

Observation, guest profile, and reference flow

I watched one guest place a pickup order on the live Jet’s Pizza site and mapped the steps from branch selection through the confirmation email. The session surfaced specific points of friction and gave me questions to test with more shoppers.

Miguel · Guest shopper persona

Orders pizza for pickup and wants to verify the total, the store, and that the order went through.

Proposed guest flow

  1. Choose a pickup branch.
  2. Select the combo and customize the items.
  3. Verify address, items, and total.
  4. Place the order and pay at pickup.
  5. Use the email confirmation to look up the order.
Competitive analysis · Jet’s Pizza vs. Domino’s

I compared the two ordering flows during the audit to see how each handled the information a guest needs before and after placing a pickup order.

What I observed in the two ordering flows
AreaJet’s PizzaDomino’s
Combo contentsThe combo photo showed breadsticks sold separately.The offer showed the items included in the deal.
PricingIncluded choices appeared with added prices in the builder.Itemized choices separated included options from paid extras.
Pickup locationThe street address disappeared during checkout.The selected store’s street address stayed visible.
ConfirmationThe email lacked the full pickup address and an order number.The branded confirmation showed store details and an order ID.

What I used in the redesign. I made inclusions explicit, kept the pickup address visible, and moved the order number to the top of the receipt.

Reflection

What I’d test next

The session showed how a missing detail can carry over from checkout to pickup. The redesign’s effect on shoppers is still an open question.

I’d give several first-time guest shoppers a pickup task. Before placing the order, I’d ask them to find the total and street address. Afterward, I’d ask them to find the order number in the email. I’d record first attempts and where anyone needed help.

Explore the order tracking concept

This optional concept follows the order from confirmation to pickup. I’d explore it after testing the core checkout and receipt.
